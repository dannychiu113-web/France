The History of Yosemite

From Native Heritage to Protected Wilderness

Origins & Historic Timeline

Native Heritage & Ahwahnee

Long before European contact, the Ahwahnechee people lived in Yosemite Valley for thousands of years, calling it "Ahwahnee" (meaning "place like a gaping mouth"). They managed the land through traditional ecological knowledge, using controlled burns to preserve open oak groves and harvest acorns as a staple food.

The Gold Rush & Conflict (1850s)

The 1849 California Gold Rush brought tens of thousands of miners into the Sierra Nevada region, leading to rapid displacement of indigenous populations and violent conflicts, including the Mariposa Indian War of 1851.

The Yosemite Grant (1864)

Recognizing the unmatched scenic beauty of the valley, President Abraham Lincoln signed the historic Yosemite Grant on June 30, 1864. This protected Yosemite Valley and the Mariposa Grove for public use, laying the foundations for the global national park idea.

National Park Status (1890)

Driven by conservationist John Muir and magazine editor Robert Underwood Johnson, Congress officially established Yosemite as a National Park on October 1, 1890, protecting over 750,000 acres of Sierra wilderness.
